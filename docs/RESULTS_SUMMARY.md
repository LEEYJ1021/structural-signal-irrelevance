# Results Summary

Consolidated statistics for RQ1-RQ3, generated by `src/analysis/` and
`src/coldstart_v5/step_m_intervention_timing_simulation.py`. This document
is meant to be the single citable source for every reported number; the
root [`README.md`](../README.md)'s §3.6 ("RQ1-RQ3 results at a glance") is
a condensed pointer to this one, reproducing the three tables below
verbatim -- cite this file, not the README, for the underlying statistics.
For the narrative of *why* each method looks the way it does, see
[`METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md`](METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md).

Sample: cold-start candidates = 250, trajectory-usable = 222, after
excluding two near-zero-spend template accounts (`config.yaml ->
sample_definition.known_test_account_ids`, see `METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md`
entry 7) = 207 ad groups / 29-30 customers, depending on the
window-completeness filter applied by each analysis (see each RQ's exact
n below).

---

## RQ1 -- Does account maturity predict initial growth slope?

**H1**: account maturity (log all-time ad-group count, standardized) is
positively associated with a newly registered ad group's initial 30-day
growth slope.

**Primary method**: customer-level aggregate OLS (HC3 robust SE) --
n = customers, not ad groups, since maturity varies only at the customer
level (Step K established this is the effective sample size; MixedLM is
structurally non-identified against it, see `METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md` entry
4). **Final arbiter**: cluster (customer-label) permutation test.

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| n (customers) | 29 |
| n (ad groups, informational) | 204 |
| OLS beta (raw scale) | 8.34 |
| OLS HC3 p-value | .576 |
| Bootstrap 95% CI (raw scale) | [-15.84, 43.08] |
| Cluster permutation p-value | .663 |
| Spearman rho | -.02 (p = .92) |
| Leave-one-out (largest customer excluded) permutation p-value | .702 (sign unchanged) |
| Winsorized (10%) OLS beta / p | 1.48 / .841 |
| Rank-rank OLS beta / p | -.02 / .924 |
| Standardized effect size (beta) | .085 |
| Pre-registered large-effect detection threshold | .50 |
| Observed effect as % of detection threshold | 16.9% |

**Verdict: H1 not supported.** Five independent checks (raw OLS,
bootstrap CI, cluster permutation, leave-one-out, Winsorized/rank-rank
OLS) agree; the small positive raw-scale coefficient is attributable to
a handful of high-leverage observations (it collapses under Winsorizing
and reverses sign under a rank transform). The standardized effect size
(.085) sits well below even the small-effect band evaluated in the Step
K power simulation, indicating this is a genuine null rather than an
under-powered detection -- enlarging the sample would be unlikely to
change the conclusion.

## RQ2 -- Do early operating signals predict later growth, and does maturity add value?

**H2a**: early-window features (coverage, spend level/slope, CTR/CVR/
ROAS) predict later-window growth. **H2b**: adding account maturity
improves on H2a.

**Method**: Ridge regression, repeated customer-grouped train/test
splits (Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the paired improvement) +
Leave-One-Customer-Out CV, decomposed into within- and
between-customer components (Step L / `METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md`, entry
5).

| early/later window (days) | n (ad groups) | H2a within-customer LOCO rho | H2b within-customer LOCO rho | within-customer improvement | repeated-split Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 / 14 | 204 | 0.467 | 0.487 | +0.019 | .038 (H2b *worse* on repeated-split rho) |
| 30 / 30 | 184 | 0.275 | 0.257 | -0.018 | .119 |
| 30 / 60 | 179 | 0.060 | 0.061 | +0.001 | .019 (H2b *worse* on repeated-split rho) |

**Verdict: H2a supported at short horizons, decaying sharply beyond
them; H2b not supported at any horizon.** 14-day-ahead prediction shows
substantial within-customer signal (rho ~ 0.39-0.47 depending on
evaluation design); this decays to near zero by the 30/60-day horizon.
Adding account maturity never produces a within-customer improvement
exceeding +0.02 at any window pair, and the repeated-split design finds
the addition significantly *harmful* at two of three window pairs.
Pooled/between-customer improvements that appear positive (e.g. +0.388
between-customer at 30/60d) are attributable to RQ1-level signal
leaking into a pooled metric, not genuine ad-group-level gain (see
`METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md`, entry 5).

## RQ3 -- At what point should a low-growth ad group be flagged for intervention?

**Method**: precision/recall/lift of early-signal-based flagging against
realized low-growth outcomes at candidate decision cutoffs (7/14/21
days), evaluated on a fixed 44-day total horizon so cutoffs share an
identical ad-group population. This is the only *measured* RQ3 result;
see below for why the accompanying expected-uplift simulation is not
reported as a policy conclusion.

| decision cutoff (days) | out-of-fold predictive rho (95% bootstrap CI) | lift @ threshold=0.25 | lift @ threshold=0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 0.304 [0.145, 0.445] | 0.83 | 1.27 |
| 14 | 0.265 [0.123, 0.404] | 1.33 | 1.23 |
| 21 | 0.334 [0.210, 0.459] | 1.42 | 1.36 |

(threshold = 0.10 excluded from reported results -- Step M6-1 found
12.6-13.1% of ad groups have `growth_target == 0`, which destabilizes
quantile cuts at this narrow a band.)

**Verdict: directional, not precise.** Flagging achieves 1.2-1.4x lift
over random across all tested cutoffs and reliable thresholds (see the
table above: the reported lifts span 0.83x-1.42x across cells, with the
reliable, non-degenerate range for both thresholds at both a lower and
upper cutoff sitting at roughly 1.2x-1.4x). The 95% bootstrap confidence
intervals on predictive rho overlap substantially across all three
cutoffs, so no single cutoff is statistically distinguishable as
"optimal" -- reported as "early judgment (within 3 weeks of
registration) is viable; a single precise optimal day is not defensible
from this sample," not as a null result. Two successive expected-uplift
simulations (delta x efficacy, then a horizon-dependent delta) were both
found to be mathematically incapable of producing a different cutoff
ranking regardless of the assumed effect size, because the assumed
parameters entered the formula as constants multiplying every (cutoff,
threshold) cell identically; this is reported as a designed-in
limitation of the uplift approach, not as a finding about intervention
effectiveness (`METHODOLOGY_NOTES.md`, entry 6).

## Combined takeaway

Initial ad-group growth is explained by the ad group's **own** early
operating signal (RQ2a), not by the parent account's accumulated
history -- at the customer level (RQ1, null), the ad-group level with
maturity added (RQ2b, null/harmful), or as an input to intervention
timing (RQ3, no differential value demonstrated across cutoffs). The
practical implication is that account size or tenure should not be used
as a proxy for how a new ad group will perform; the ad group's own first
two weeks of activity is the more informative -- and, at this sample
size, the *only* reliably informative -- signal.
